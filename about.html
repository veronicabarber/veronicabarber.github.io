About Me

I am a first-generation student from Northeastern New York; of the Kanienʼkehá꞉ka. My intellectual life began with Thoreau's Walden and his accompanying transcendentalism and Nietzsche’s idea of amor fati. I am a believer of hope and minimalism; toward a labor of common respect and understanding. In political theory I wonder how this can translate to answer for pluralistic needs without flattening them.

Through theese interests, I have come to explore the realm of artificial intelligence through both it's pro's and con's. Currently, I am researching the repercussions of AI's continued advancement on society and government. Alongside the theory, I am drawn to technology governance--machine learning, AI alignment, and most recently, the development of contained retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems.

Academically, I am currently examining the use of AI in public decision-making and what it means for democratic legitimacy. At stake is whether we can rely on artificial intelligence to aid collective decisions without losing our humanism, and why that even matters.
